## Summary

At 1299 qubits two phase families bind the peak: the dialog-GCD walk-body
phases of the compressed transcript blocks, and the final chunk of the
apply-phase modular add. This commit lowers both by one.

- **GCD body.** It adds two scratch-lifetime levers that are exact:
  - the body's composite scratch may now borrow transcript cells of later
    slots in the same block, which are still |0> at that moment;
  - the body's own freshly allocated scratch is released right after the
    subtract instead of after the shift.

  With these in place the step-11 binder notch of [f3820d0](f3820d0.md) is
  no longer needed. It is replaced by a 1-bit notch at step 13.
- **Apply.** The last apply chunk cut moves from bit 189 to bit 190. The
  final chunk, which loads its slice of the addend into fresh wires, is one
  bit narrower.

The extra boundary-replay bit costs about half a Toffoli per apply call, so
+259 Toffoli in total, for one qubit, worth about 1,081 Toffoli of score here.

## What changed and why it saves qubits

1. **`DIALOG_GCD_BORROW_ZERO_RAW_FUTURE=1`** (new
   `dialog_gcd_borrow_zero_raw_future_enabled()`, used in
   `dialog_gcd_build_composite_scratch`, `rounds/dialog/compressed.rs`).
   - The walk writes its branch and shift bits into a raw transcript block
     of `group_size` slots, with 2 cells per slot plus, under K2, a third
     cell (`s2`) per slot at `raw_block[2·group_size + slot]`.
   - At step `step`, the slots after `slot = step % group_size` are not
     written yet in the forward pass, and they were already uncomputed in
     the reverse pass. Their cells are |0>.
   - The new block appends those cells to the scratch `lanes`, skipping
     any cell that is already a lane or lies inside `u[..active_width]` or
     `v[..active_width]`, until `want = 2·body_len − 1` lanes are reached.
     Only the remainder is allocated fresh (`owned`). Fewer owned wires
     means a lower peak in the body phases. See
     [venting-and-wire-loans](../concepts/venting-and-wire-loans.md).
   - The `PROBE_SCRATCH` debug print now reports `lanes.len()`.
2. **`DIALOG_GCD_FREE_SCRATCH_BEFORE_SHIFT=1`**, in
   `emit_dialog_gcd_compressed_sidecar_tobitvector_steps_block_lifecycle`.
   The owned scratch was freed after the shift and the K2 conditional
   second shift. It is now freed right after
   `dialog_gcd_controlled_sub_selected`. The subtract has already returned
   those wires to |0>, so the shift and cswap phases run with them gone.
3. **Binder notch retune** in `configure_ecdsafail_submission_route()`.
   - `DIALOG_GCD_BINDER_NOTCH_STEPS`: `8,9,10,11` → `8,9,10`. Step 11 keeps
     only its band trim 3 and the default trio notch +2, so its body is 2
     bits wider again.
   - New `DIALOG_GCD_BINDER_NOTCH_MAP=13:1`, read by the new
     `dialog_gcd_binder_notch_map_extra(step)` in `rounds/dialog/mod.rs`.
     It is a per-step list `step:extra` added on top of the uniform notch.
     Step 13's body goes from 251 to 250 bits (active width 254, band trim 3).
   - Toffoli: about +4 per body at step 11 and −2 at step 13, over 4 walks,
     about +8.
4. **`DIALOG_GCD_APPLY_CHUNKED_F_CUT4`: 189 → 190.**
   - The apply add `dialog_gcd_add_ctrl_chunked_low_to_ext` (and its
     subtract twin) splits the 257-bit extended sum into chunks at
     50/100/150/`F_CUT4`. The final chunk `[F_CUT4, 257)` gets its addend
     slice loaded into fresh wires (`dialog_gcd_load_controlled_slice`).
     Moving the cut up by one shrinks that load by one wire, which lowers
     the final-chunk phase that co-bound the peak.
   - Cost: the measured boundary clear of [bfd3fa6](bfd3fa6.md) now
     replays the segment `[150, 190)` instead of `[150, 189)`. That is one
     more conditioned CCX, which runs on about half the shots: about
     +0.5 × 516 calls ≈ +258. With item 3, about +266. The measured
     average is +259, and the difference is draw variance.
   - The author notes that `F_CUT4 = 196` also reached 1298 but cost too
     much Toffoli.
5. **Dormant knob `DIALOG_GCD_TOBITVECTOR_SHIFT_BODY_TRIM`** (new
   `dialog_gcd_tobitvector_shift_width`). When set to 1, the walk shift and
   unshift loops would run over the body width only. It is not pinned, so
   `shift_width = active_width`, and the loops over `v[..shift_width]` are
   identical to the old `v_active` loops.
6. **Classical nonce filter** (`dialog_gcd_classical_filter.rs`):
   - `body_carry_extra_notch` also reads `DIALOG_GCD_BINDER_NOTCH_MAP`.
   - New `HardReason::BodyTrimMismatch`. In `truncated_gcd_step`, on a
     subtract step the filter computes `v − u` both on the full active
     window and on the trimmed body width (`sub_low_window`), and marks the
     input hard if they differ inside the active window. This makes the
     filter screen out the body-truncation hazard that the notches create.
7. **`DIALOG_TAIL_NONCE`: 45892140 → 47863838.**

## Why it is still correct, and what it risks

- **Exact:**
  - The zero-cell borrow is exact, because the borrowed cells are |0> at
    that moment and the measured add/sub body returns them to |0> before
    their own slot writes them.
  - Freeing the owned scratch earlier is exact, because those wires are
    already clean after the subtract.
  - The cut move is exact, because the chunked add plus the exact
    boundary clear is value-exact for any cut.
  - The two dormant knobs are off.
- **Probabilistic:** the step-13 one-bit notch. The route's own comment on
  body trims records that a 1-bit body truncation "mis-drops a needed bit
  when the converged operand bitlen reaches active_width on a handful of
  reachable inputs". The new `BodyTrimMismatch` filter check exists for
  exactly this. A failure shows as a classical mismatch and ancilla garbage.
- Un-notching step 11 removes one such hazard.
- The shipped nonce `47863838` draws a test set with 0 classical, 0 phase
  and 0 ancilla failures over all 9,024 shots. See
  [nonce-island-and-lambda](../concepts/nonce-island-and-lambda.md).

## Classification rationale

The qubit comes from scratch lifetime and placement: borrowing idle clean
cells, freeing owned scratch earlier, and shrinking a freshly loaded chunk.
That is scratch/ancilla reuse. The notch moves are a smaller
width-truncation component.
