## Summary

The 2309-qubit peak had been a wall for every contestant. Three clusters sat
within two qubits of it: the Kaliski inversion sweep, the affine
`y`-multiply and pair1's first multiply. This commit lowers all of them
together with three levers, all on by default:

1. **Dialog history-fold** (`KAL_DIALOG_FOLD`). The Kaliski inversion keeps
   one history qubit `m_hist[i]` per iteration. These bits now live in the high
   bits of the GCD register `v_w`, which are known to be |0⟩ once `v_w` has
   shrunk, instead of in a separate register. This is a relabelling of qubit
   ids and costs no gates.
2. **Carry-pool relocation, "C\*"** (`KAL_GZ_EARLY_RECOVER`). The wide STEP-4
   fast-Cuccaro adds used future `m_hist` slots as their carry register, and
   that pinned those slots so they could not be folded. Their carries now go
   first on the high bits of `s`, `r` and `u`, which are also |0⟩ at that
   moment. This frees about twice as many history slots for the fold (196
   instead of about 99).
3. **Affine square-recompute** (`AFFINE_SQUARE_RECOMPUTE`). The 512-qubit λ²
   product used to stay alive across the affine `y`-multiply, which holds its
   own 512-qubit product. It is now uncomputed right after use and computed
   again later.

The peak drops by 284 qubits to 2025. Toffoli rises by 151,742 (+6.3%),
about +65k of it from the extra square pair
(the code comment's estimate). Score is Q × T, so the trade pays: 2309/2025
is a 12.3% qubit cut for a 6.3% Toffoli rise. The `KAL_REROLL` tail goes from
35 to 43 to land a clean draw.

## What changed and why it saves qubits

**Background.** Both inversions run through `with_kal_inv_raw_borrow_v_w_pair`
(`src/point_add/kaliski_inv.rs`). It aliases the live denominator register
`tx` as the GCD register `v_w`, and allocates `u`, `r`, `s` (256 each),
`m_hist` (one qubit per iteration, 399 or 397) and `f_flag`. In STEP 4 of each
iteration the walk does `v_w −= tmp` and `s += tmp` with the measurement-based
fast Cuccaro. That adder needs a clean |0⟩ carry register of width − 1. Before
this commit the carry register was "borrowed" from `m_future = m_hist[i+1..]`,
the history slots of later iterations, which are still |0⟩
(`sub_nbit_qq_fast_mfut` and friends in `cuccaro.rs`). Any shortfall was
allocated fresh, or the adder fell back to the slow in-place Cuccaro if the
shortfall exceeded 131. The carries are erased by measurement, so the
borrowed bits come back as |0⟩. See
[venting-and-wire-loans](../concepts/venting-and-wire-loans.md) and
[measurement-uncompute](../concepts/measurement-uncompute.md).

### 1. Dialog history-fold (`kaliski_state.rs`, `kaliski_inv.rs`, `kaliski_walk.rs`)

- `kal_dialog_fold_enabled()` reads `KAL_DIALOG_FOLD`, default on.
- `dialog_fold_vw_slotmap(n, iters)` decides, for each history slot `j`,
  whether it can live at a coordinate `c` of `v_w` (`Some(c)`) or needs a
  dedicated qubit (`None`).
  - `wt_hi(i) = kal_wtrunc_width(i − 1) + slack` is the highest bit of `v_w`
    that iteration `i` can touch. It uses `i − 1` to cover the merged
    `(u, v_w)` cswap carried over from the previous iteration. `slack` is
    `kal_dialog_fold_slack()`, default 4.
  - `anchor(j)` is the largest `wt_hi(i)` over the iteration that writes slot
    `j` (`i = j`) and over every iteration `i` that borrows slot `j` as a carry,
    meaning `j` is in `[i+1, i+drawn(i)]`. `drawn(i)` is the part of the STEP-4
    carry need that is not covered by the clean pools of lever 2.
  - A slot is foldable when `anchor(j) < n`. The slots are sorted by anchor,
    and each takes the smallest free coordinate `≥ anchor`, which is the
    optimal greedy for this interval matching. `KAL_FOLD_DEBUG` prints the
    counts.
- In `with_kal_inv_raw_borrow_v_w_pair`, `m_hist[j]` becomes `alias_v_w[c]`
  for folded slots, and a fresh qubit otherwise. At the end only the dedicated
  qubits are freed. The allocation order is kept, so fold off is
  byte-identical.
- The STEP-6 shift `v_w >>= 1` (four sites: forward and backward, generic and
  bulk bodies) now runs only over `dialog_fold_shift_width(i) =
  kal_wtrunc_width(i) + slack` bits, so it never moves the folded history
  bits. Swaps cost no Toffoli. The slack is there because, per the comment,
  rare inputs carry a set bit one place above `kal_wtrunc_width(i)`, and the
  full shift used to bring it back into range.

### 2. Carry-pool relocation C\* (`builder.rs`, `kaliski_walk.rs`)

- `gz_early_recover()` reads `KAL_GZ_EARLY_RECOVER`, default on, under the
  existing `KAL_GOUZIEN_9N` master switch.
- Clean donor pools at iteration `i`:
  - `gz_s_clean_high(s, i)` and `gz_r_clean_high(r, i)` are `s[i+1..n]` and
    `r[i+1..n]`. The Bézout coefficients at most double per iteration, so
    `bitlen ≤ i + 1` at the STEP-4 instant (the step-3 `(r, s)` cswap is
    `i + 1` wide). This is a proof, not a fit.
  - `gz_u_clean_high_wtrunc(u, i)` is `u[gz_load_width(i)..n]`, with
    `gz_load_width(i) = min(i < n ? n : 2n − i, kal_wtrunc_width(i))`. These
    bits are zero by the W-TRUNC envelope, the same empirical bound the STEP-4
    load already assumes.
- `gz_vw_clean_pool = s-high ++ r-high ++ u-high` hosts the `v_w −= tmp` SUB
  (forward) and `v_w += tmp` ADD (backward). `gz_s_clean_pool = r-high ++ u-high`
  hosts the `s += tmp` add and `s −= tmp` sub (`s` is the accumulator there).
  Both are passed as the first pool to `*_nbit_qq_fast_mfut_pool`, with
  `m_future` second, at all eight sites (forward and backward, generic and
  bulk bodies).
- Effect: early iterations, where `v_w` is wide and has no idle high bits,
  no longer pull hundreds of carries out of `m_future`. Those history slots
  stop being pinned and can fold. The author reports the fold going from about
  99 to 196 slots, and the inversion sweep's local peak from 2210 to about 2026.
  The gates are the same adders on different wires, so the Toffoli count of
  this lever is about zero.

### 3. Affine square-recompute (`mul_affine.rs`, `square_tx_and_combined_ty_l2minus3qx`)

- Old path: build `tmp_ext = λ²` (512 qubits), fold it into a 256-bit `breg`,
  run the `y`-multiply (which allocates its own 512-bit product), use
  `tmp_ext` again to zero `breg`, and only then uncompute `tmp_ext`. The two
  512-bit products were live together, and the affine phases peaked at 2307.
- New path (`AFFINE_SQUARE_RECOMPUTE`, default on):
  1. Square into `tmp_ext`.
  2. Fold into `breg` with the new `mod_add_solinas_ext_product_clean_lowscratch`.
     This uses the register-free `mod_*_qq_lowq_lowscratch` position folds,
     `mod_double_inplace_direct` doublings and the plain shift22, and restores
     `tmp_ext` exactly.
  3. Uncompute the square at once and free the 512 qubits.
  4. Run `breg −= 3·ox`, the `y`-multiply, `breg += 3·ox`, then `tx −= breg`.
  5. Square again into a fresh `tmp_ext2`, zero `breg` with the matching `_sub_`
     fold, and uncompute.
- The squares use the new `schoolbook_square_symmetric_pb(_inverse)` with
  `max_fast_width = AFFINE_SQUARE_RECOMPUTE_MFW = 243`. Rows wider than 243
  bits (the first 14) use the slow in-place `cuccaro_add`/`cuccaro_sub`, which
  needs no carry register, instead of the fast one. This keeps the affine
  phases at 2024, one below the new 2025 binder. A dirty-borrow fold variant
  (`AFFINE_RECOMPUTE_DIRTY_FOLD`, default off) is also added. The comment says
  it is unsound on this layout.
- The extra square pair is what the Toffoli pays for. The comment estimates
  it at about +65k. The rest of the +151,742 is not broken down by the author.
  It is presumably the slower register-free adders of the clean folds and of
  the clamped rows on this path.

### 4. Re-roll (`mod.rs`)

`KAL_REROLL` default 35 → 43 (43 X;X pairs on `tx[0]`). The comment says 40
is an equally clean alternative.

The new peak is a multi-cluster wall: `shift22_step4` inside pair1's first
multiply at 2025, and the affine recompute clusters at 2024. See
[peak-qubits-and-budgets](../concepts/peak-qubits-and-budgets.md).

## Why it is still correct, and what it risks

- **Exact parts.** The fold and C\* only change which wires hold values and
  carries. Each borrowed wire is |0⟩ when borrowed and is returned to |0⟩ by
  the same measurement uncompute, and the forward and backward passes use the
  same qubit ids, so the circuit stays reversible. The `s`/`r` high bits are
  provably zero. The affine recompute is value-identical: the author reports it
  is clean with all truncations switched off.
- **New dependence on W-TRUNC.** The `u`-high carry donors and the folded
  `v_w` bits are only |0⟩ if the input respects the W-TRUNC envelope (margin 0
  here). An input that exceeds the envelope by more than the 4-bit slack can
  now also corrupt a history bit or a borrowed carry. Such inputs already
  failed under W-TRUNC, but the failure is now more likely to show up as phase
  or ancilla garbage than as a plain classical mismatch.
- **Truncated STEP-6 shift.** It is exact while `v_w < 2^(width + slack)`. The
  slack of 4 keeps the one-bit excursions the comment describes.
- **Island.** The new op stream re-draws the test set. The shipped
  `KAL_REROLL = 43` draws a clean set: 0 classical, 0 phase and 0 ancilla
  failures over 9,024 shots. See
  [nonce-island-and-lambda](../concepts/nonce-island-and-lambda.md).

## Classification rationale

All three levers lower the peak by changing where and for how long values are
held: history bits move into idle wires, carries are loaned from known-zero
wires, and the λ² product is freed early and recomputed. That is ancilla-lifetime /
venting under Register and Scratch Allocation. The recompute could be read as
Uncompute Strategy → recompute-later, but it is the smallest of the three
qubit levers.

+/// v_w HIGH-bit coord hosting `m_hist[j]` (`Some(c)` => fold into `v_w[c]`), or

+/// `max(load_width(i), add_width(i)) - 1`, capped by the live `m_future` length.

+        return Vec::new();
