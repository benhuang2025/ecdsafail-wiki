## Summary

Every replay cell of the ping-pong divider adds two 256-bit registers in
three chunks of 86, 85 and 85 bits (`add_chunked_measured`, chunk limit 96). The carry between two chunks is kept
on a wire and later measured out with a phase repair. In the base, all
boundary carries were held until the whole add was done, and the add's final
carry-out wire was allocated before the first chunk. So the last chunk ran
with both boundary carries and the carry-out live.

This commit reorders the same gates. Each boundary carry is erased right
after the next chunk has used it, and the carry-out is allocated only when
the last chunk needs it. At most one boundary carry is pending at a time, so
the chunked add needs one wire less. Separately, new exact helpers skip the
low carry positions of the small constant folds that are provably always 0.
At 1317 qubits the peak had two owners: the multiply replay's chunked add
and the round-0 endpoint fold of the divide replay. The reorder lowers the
first and the dead-low helper lowers the second, so together they give
1317 → 1316 qubits and −27 Toffoli.

## What changed and why it saves qubits

### 1. Rescheduled chunked add (`src/point_add/pingpong_div.rs`)

- **New `erase_boundary_carry(b, carry, addend, acc, lo, hi)`.** It is the
  old erase body moved into a function:
  `hmr(carry, phase)`, then
  `cmp_lt_phase_conditioned(&acc[hi − k..hi], &addend[hi − k..hi], phase)`
  with `k = min(replay_chunk_compare(), hi − lo)`, then `free(carry)`.
- **`add_chunked_measured(b, addend, acc, want_carry_out: bool) -> Option<QubitId>`**
  replaces the version that took a pre-allocated `carry_out: Option<QubitId>`.
  - The loop over the chunks [0,86), [86,171), [171,256) keeps one
    `pending` boundary.
  - After `chunk_add` for chunk `k`, it erases chunk `k − 1`'s boundary
    carry, which chunk `k` has just consumed as its carry-in.
  - The carry-out is allocated inside the loop, only for the last chunk and
    only if `want_carry_out`.
  - Old order, for comparison: allocate carry-out → chunk 0 → chunk 1 →
    chunk 2 → erase boundary 1 → erase boundary 0.
- **Live wires at the widest point.** During the last chunk the base held
  boundary 0, boundary 1 and the carry-out. Now boundary 0 is already gone.
- **Callers.** The three callers (`signed_mod_add_pm_halve_fused`,
  `signed_mod_double_add_pm_fused`, `signed_mod_add_pm`) change from
  `let overflow = b.alloc_qubit(); add_chunked_measured(.., Some(overflow))`
  to `let overflow = add_chunked_measured(.., true).expect(..)`.
- **Why the early erase is the same repair.** The doc comment gives the
  argument. The repair reads only `acc[hi − k..hi]` and `addend[hi − k..hi]`.
  `acc` in chunk `k`'s range is final as soon as chunk `k`'s add returns,
  because later chunks write strictly higher positions. `chunk_add` also
  restores `addend` exactly. So the emitted repair is identical whether it
  runs right away or at the end. It only moves earlier in the stream.
- **Peak.** The doc comment says the two scheduling changes "reduce peak
  width without changing a gate". The replay cell runs while the whole tape
  and both coefficient registers are live. A memory note added a few hours
  later by `152fbe0` (`memory/07-pingpong-liveness.md`, on the same base
  `adec6f4`) measured the chunked add's live ladder at 87/87/87 wires per
  chunk before the reorder and 86/86/86 after. The multiply cell holds one
  extra wire (`doubled_out`), so it sat at the 1317 peak before and at 1316
  after. See
  [venting-and-wire-loans](../concepts/venting-and-wire-loans.md)
  ("Rescheduling") and [pingpong-division](../concepts/pingpong-division.md).

### 2. Dead-low carry elision (`src/point_add/arith/const_arith.rs`)

- New `dead_low_carry_run(c, last, first_carry_is_zero)` returns how many
  low carry positions are identically 0.
  - The ripple is `k[i] = MAJ(a_i, c_i·ctrl, k[i−1])` with `k[−1] = 0`.
  - `k[0]` is 0 if the constant's bit 0 is clear, or if the caller passes a
    proof that it is 0.
  - After that, every clear constant bit extends the run, because
    `k[i] = a_i·0 = 0`.
- New `cadd_nbit_const_direct_trunc_fast_dead_low` and
  `csub_nbit_const_direct_trunc_fast_dead_low` allocate carries only for
  positions `dead..=last`, and skip their CCX and their `hmr`/`cz_if`
  erasure.
- **Uses in `pingpong_div.rs`**, with `f = 2^32 + 977` (bits 1–3 clear) and
  `f − 1` (bits 0–3 clear):
  - `conditional_mod_negate` subtracts `f − 1`: 4 dead borrows from the
    constant alone.
  - `mod_halve_pm` subtracts `f` with `first_borrow_is_zero = true`, because
    the control `parity` is a copy of `target[0]`, so `!a_0·ctrl = 0`.
  - `mod_double_pm` adds `f` with `first_carry_is_zero = true`, because the
    shift has left `target[0] = 0`.
  - `seed_round_one` and `seed_round_one_inverse` use `f − 1` with
    window 32.
- Each call saves 3–4 CCX on every shot. Over the roughly ten calls per
  point addition (two traversals) that is about −30 T. The measured total is
  −27. See [adders-and-carries](../concepts/adders-and-carries.md).
- **It also matters for the peak.** The same `152fbe0` note found that at
  1317 the other peak owner was the round-0 `mod_halve_pm` endpoint chain of
  the divide replay: 87 borrow ancillas plus `parity`, 88 wires. Here that
  chain loses its 4 dead borrows, so it drops below the chunked add. Without
  this, the reorder alone would have left the peak at 1317.

### 3. Tail nonce (`src/point_add/mod.rs`)

The default `SUB4_PINGPONG_TAIL_NONCE` goes from 165193 to 191412.

## Why it is still correct, and what it risks

- **The rescheduling is exact.** The same gates are emitted in a different
  order, and every moved repair reads only bits that are already final. The
  truncated boundary compare keeps its existing approximation (a top-`k` tie
  gives a wrong phase), unchanged in rate.
- **The dead-low elision is exact.** Every skipped gate writes 0 into a wire
  that is 0 on every basis state. The two call-site facts are structural:
  `parity` is a CX copy of `target[0]`, and the shift clears `target[0]`.
- So the commit adds no new hard inputs. It still re-rolls the test draw.
- The shipped nonce `191412` draws a clean test set: 0 classical, 0 phase and
  0 ancilla failures. See
  [nonce-island-and-lambda](../concepts/nonce-island-and-lambda.md).

## Classification rationale

The score is driven by the −1 qubit, and the main move is shortening
scratch-wire lifetimes without changing any gate. That is ancilla-lifetime /
venting. The dead-low carry elision, the kind of exact trim the census files
as host-derived / classical specialization, is the runner-up: it is needed to
clear the second peak owner and gives the −27 T.
