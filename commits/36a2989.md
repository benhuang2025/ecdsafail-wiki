## Summary

Each Kaliski iteration swaps `u` and `v_w` under its branch bit `a_k` at
STEP 3, does its arithmetic, and swaps them back under the same bit at STEP 9.
The next iteration then swaps under `a_{k+1}` at its STEP 3. Two controlled
swaps in a row compose: `cswap(a_k) · cswap(a_{k+1}) = cswap(a_k ⊕ a_{k+1})`.
This commit skips STEP 9's `(u, v_w)` swap and does one swap under
`a_k ⊕ a_{k+1}` at the next STEP 3. The same merge already existed for the
`(r, s)` pair, so the "frame" qubit that carries `a_k` across the boundary was
already there.

A 256-wide controlled swap costs 256 Toffoli. Removing one per iteration in
the first 253 iterations of all four Kaliski runs (forward and backward of both
pairs) saves about 253 × 256 × 4 ≈ 259,000 Toffoli. The fix-ups cost 4 CCX per
iteration. Pair2 also moves from 399 to 398 iterations to land a clean draw.
Qubits are unchanged.

## What changed and why it saves Toffoli

Background on the Kaliski iteration and the bulk prefix: see
[`a3ee589`](a3ee589.md). In the bulk iteration
(`kaliski_iteration_bulk_prefix3` in `src/point_add/kaliski_walk.rs`):
- STEP 1 computes `a_f = !u[0]` and `m_i = u[0] & !v_w[0]` and `b_f = a_f ⊕ m_i`;
- STEP 2 computes `l_gt = (u > v_w)` and updates `a_f`, `m_i`;
- STEP 3 swaps `(u, v_w)` and `(r, s)` under `a_f`;
- STEPs 4 to 8 do the conditional subtract, the halving of `v_w` and the
  doubling of `r`;
- STEP 9 swaps both pairs back under `a_f`.

The existing `(r, s)` merge (`kal_cswap_rs_merge_enabled()`, default on)
already deferred the `(r, s)` STEP 9: it copies `a_k` into a fresh `frame`
qubit, resets `a_f` from it, and lets the next iteration's STEP 3 swap under
`frame ⊕ a_{k+1}` and then reset `frame` from `r[0]`/`s[0]`. `frame` is
allocated after STEP 8 and freed at the next STEP 3, so it is never live at
the STEP 4 peak.

### New knobs (`src/point_add/kaliski_state.rs`)

- `kal_cswap_uv_merge_enabled()`: on unless `KAL_CSWAP_UV_MERGE=0`.
- `kal_cswap_uv_merge_safe_iters()`: 254 unless overridden by
  `KAL_CSWAP_UV_MERGE_SAFE_ITERS`.

### Forward bulk iteration

With `merge_uv = merge_rs && kal_cswap_uv_merge_enabled()`:
- `uv_merge_out = merge_uv && !is_last && iter_idx + 1 < 254`: STEP 9 skips the
  `(u, v_w)` cswap loop. The physical `u, v_w` are left exchanged when
  `a_k = 1`.
- `uv_merge_in = merge_uv && iter_idx < 254`: the next iteration receives
  `frame = a_{k-1}` and has to work on registers that may be exchanged.
  - **STEP 1 fix-up.** `a_f` and `m_i` were computed from the physical bit 0s.
    If the registers are exchanged and `u[0] ≠ v_w[0]`, both values are wrong
    by exactly one flip, because `!u0` vs `!v0` differ, and exactly one of
    `u0 & !v0`, `v0 & !u0` is 1. If `u[0] = v_w[0]`, the exchange changes
    nothing. So the fix is to toggle both on `frame & (u0 ⊕ v0)`:
    `cx(v_w[0], u[0]); ccx(frame, u[0], a_f); ccx(frame, u[0], m_i); cx(v_w[0], u[0])`.
    That is 2 CCX.
  - **STEP 2 fix-up.** `with_gt` computes the physical `u > v_w`. If the
    registers are exchanged and `u ≠ v_w`, the canonical answer is the
    opposite. So `l_gt ^= frame` is applied inside the compare and undone
    before the compare uncomputes. This costs only CX, but it is only right
    when `u ≠ v_w`.
  - **STEP 3.** One swap loop under `a_f ⊕ frame`
    (`cx(frame, a_f); cswap loop; cx(frame, a_f)`) replaces the deferred STEP 9
    swap plus this STEP 3 swap. After it, the registers are in the canonical
    post-STEP-3 order, so STEPs 4 to 8 are unchanged.

### Backward bulk iteration

`kaliski_iteration_bulk_prefix3_backward` mirrors the forward run:
- the reverse STEP 9 `(u, v_w)` loop is skipped when `uv_merge_out`;
- the reverse STEP 3 does the `(u, v_w)` loop inside the merged
  `frame_out ⊕ a_f` block, and skips its separate eager loop;
- the STEP 2 and STEP 1 fix-ups are applied with the re-created `frame_out`,
  in reverse order.

### Why the saving is this large

A cswap is one Toffoli per bit. The `(u, v_w)` swap is 256 wide for every
iteration below 256 (`uv_width = n` while `iter_idx < n`). Iterations 0 to 252
emit their STEP 9 swap in merged form, in each of four runs, forward and
backward, so the removed swaps alone are 253 × 4 × 256 ≈ 259,000 Toffoli. The
new STEP 1 fix-ups add 2 CCX per merged iteration, forward and backward.

### Pair2 retune

`src/point_add/point_add.rs`, `pair2_default`: 399 → 398. The comment records
that 397 and 399 each give one classical mismatch on this op stream, and 398
is clean. One fewer pair2 iteration saves about 3,500 Toffoli.

## Why it is still correct, and what it risks

- **The swap merge is exact.** Two controlled swaps of the same pair compose
  to one swap under the XOR of the controls. The frame qubit is uncomputed
  exactly as in the `(r, s)` merge.
- **The STEP 1 fix-up is exact.** It is a case check on `u0, v0`.
- **The STEP 2 fix-up needs `u ≠ v_w`.** Since `gcd(u, v_w) = 1` all the way
  through, `u = v_w` can only mean `u = v_w = 1`, so `u + v_w = 2`. The code's
  proof in the comment above `BULK_PREFIX_SAFE_ITERS` gives `u + v_w ≥ s0 / 2^i`
  with `s0 = p + dx > 2^255`, so at iteration `i ≤ 254` the sum is above 2,
  and equality cannot occur. The prefix 254 (iterations 0 to 253 take the
  fix-up) is therefore inside the provable range. The author found 254 by a
  9,024-shot sweep, and the result matches this bound. A larger prefix would
  apply the wrong STEP 2 decision on an input that reaches `(1, 1)` early. That
  would desynchronise the history bits and show as a classical mismatch or
  phase garbage.
- **Pair2 = 398 is empirical.** An input that needs more than 398 Kaliski
  steps leaves λ uncleared.
- There is no tail nonce in this era. The test set is a hash of the op stream,
  and this stream's draw is clean: 0 classical, 0 phase and 0 ancilla
  failures. See [nonce-island-and-lambda](../concepts/nonce-island-and-lambda.md).

## Classification rationale

Two consecutive controlled swaps across an iteration boundary are fused into
one: Fusion → boundary cswap-merge. The pair2 change is a secondary retune.
